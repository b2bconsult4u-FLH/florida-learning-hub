Today in Florida History · November 22, 1947

Florida Broadcasters Plan to Carry the Everglades Dedication

By Wm. E. McMullen II

On November 22, 1947, Glenn Marshall Jr. wrote to Florida broadcasters about a ceremony still two weeks away. As president of the Florida Association of Broadcasters, he wanted stations across the state to carry the dedication of Everglades National Park. His letter gave the date, December 6, and a half-hour slot in which stations could join the broadcast. It also described the technical problem of getting the program from Everglades City to listeners across Florida. [1]

Marshall explained that limited telephone facilities made it impractical for more than one broadcast feed to originate at the site. The Mutual Broadcasting System was already preparing to carry the ceremony. Florida stations could connect through Mutual’s facilities or rebroadcast the program through an existing station where possible. That way, several stations could carry the same event without each needing a separate line from Everglades City. [1]

The letter lists stations or markets across Florida and names several expected speakers. President Harry S. Truman was still a possibility in Marshall’s November letter. The National Park Service records what ultimately happened: Truman formally dedicated the park in Everglades City on December 6. The planning letter and the park history preserve different stages in the same event. [1] [2]

The park was the result of years of conservation work. Radio gave organizers a way to share the dedication with people who could not be there, including listeners far from the Everglades. The November 22 letter makes that work visible. Before speeches could be heard statewide, stations had to agree on a program, a time and a way to carry the sound. [1] [2]
