Miami’s Tobacco Road Celebrates a Claimed Century

By Wm. E. McMullen II

On November 17, 2012, Miami’s Tobacco Road celebrated what many patrons believed was its hundredth anniversary. Its story held that a liquor license dated to 1912 and made the establishment the oldest of its kind in South Florida. A birthday party is good evidence that people treasured the story. The history of a business calls for a look at other records as well. [1]

Writing in the historical journal Tequesta, Casey Piket checked the celebrated date against property and local history records. He reported that the property appraiser’s information placed the building’s existence no earlier than 1916. He also pointed to a complication for anyone tracing an early liquor license: Dade County voted to prohibit alcohol in 1913, and the change took effect in January 1914. The date a building appeared, the date a business opened in it, and the date someone obtained a license are different facts. [1]

The site still has a story worth telling. Piket follows the premises through an earlier life as a bakery and later identities that changed with Miami. That approach gives the building a history broader than the name by which its last customers knew it. Tobacco Road had closed by 2014, but the celebration remains a record of the place the business held in the city’s memory. [1]

A careful history can preserve that memory and identify exactly what it documents. November 17 is the anniversary of the 2012 celebration. A dated property record helps establish when a structure appeared, while a genuine license would help establish who was authorized to sell alcohol and when. The centennial claim makes a good question for a historian because each part can be checked separately. [1]

Classroom connection

What evidence would you look for to establish when a business first opened? How would that differ from evidence of when the building was constructed?
