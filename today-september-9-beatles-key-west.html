Today in Florida History · September 9

Hurricane Dora Sends the Beatles to Key West

September 9, 1964 — At about 3:30 in the morning, one of the most famous musical groups in the world unexpectedly landed in Key West.

The Beatles were supposed to be headed for Jacksonville.

Instead, Hurricane Dora changed the flight plan—and gave the Florida Keys one of the strangest and most memorable detours in its modern history.

John Lennon, Paul McCartney, George Harrison and Ringo Starr had left Montreal during their first major North American tour. Their destination was northeast Florida, where they were scheduled to perform at Jacksonville's Gator Bowl. But Dora was bearing down on Florida's Atlantic coast, making Jacksonville an unsafe destination.

The aircraft turned south.

Key West suddenly became part of Beatlemania.

1964 — The Year Beatlemania Reached America

By September 1964, the Beatles were no longer simply a successful British band.

Their February appearance on The Ed Sullivan Show had helped ignite an extraordinary wave of American popularity. Their records dominated the charts, teenagers followed their movements, and police departments had to plan for enormous crowds wherever the group appeared.

Their 1964 tour carried them through major cities across the United States and Canada. On September 8 they performed in Montreal before preparing to fly south toward Jacksonville.

Florida was supposed to be another concert stop.

The weather had other plans.

Hurricane Dora Blocks the Way

Hurricane Dora was approaching northeast Florida from the Atlantic.

The storm would make landfall near St. Augustine on September 10 and became one of the most significant hurricanes in northeast Florida history. As the Beatles' aircraft headed toward Florida, Jacksonville was increasingly threatened by the storm. Their flight was diverted away from the hurricane and toward Key West, more than 300 miles from the scheduled destination.

For the people aboard the airplane, Key West was essentially a weather diversion.

For Key West, it was about to become an event.

Somehow, Key West Found Out

The arrival was not part of a publicity campaign.

There was no scheduled Key West concert.

There was no planned public appearance.

Yet word spread.

According to research by the Florida Keys History Center, more than 700 fans gathered at Key West International Airport. Cars crowded the airport grounds toward South Roosevelt Boulevard. Key West police were reinforced by sheriff's deputies, the Florida Highway Patrol and Navy shore patrol personnel.

This was the middle of the night.

The Beatles had not even planned to be there.

3:30 A.M. — September 9

The aircraft touched down at approximately 3:30 a.m.

John, Paul, George and Ringo emerged into something they had become accustomed to seeing almost everywhere they traveled: excited fans desperate for a glimpse of them.

But this time the location was extraordinary.

They were hundreds of miles from their scheduled destination, on a small island at the end of the Florida Keys, because a hurricane had blocked their path to Jacksonville.

From the airport, the Beatles were taken a short distance along South Roosevelt Boulevard to the Key Wester Motel.

They had arrived in the Florida Keys.

Florida Memory Preserves the Visit

The State Archives of Florida preserves photographs of the Beatles during their unexpected Key West stay. Photographer Robert Knowles captured John Lennon, Paul McCartney, George Harrison and Ringo Starr outside their bungalow at the Key Wester Motel.

An Unexpected Day Off

The Beatles had been moving almost continuously through an exhausting schedule of concerts, airplanes, hotels, interviews and screaming crowds.

The hurricane diversion unexpectedly gave them something rare:

time with nowhere they were required to perform.

They stayed at the Key Wester Motel. Fans gathered nearby hoping to catch another glimpse of them, while security tried to maintain some separation between the musicians and the public.

For a brief moment, one of the most famous bands on earth was essentially stranded by a hurricane at the end of the Florida Keys.

The Key Wester Motel

The motel stood on South Roosevelt Boulevard, close to the airport and Atlantic shoreline.

Florida Memory's collection contains several photographs of the Beatles there as well as historical photographs of the Key Wester itself. The building eventually disappeared, but its brief association with the Beatles became part of Key West's cultural history.

Dora Was No Minor Storm

From sixty years' distance, the story can sound almost whimsical: A hurricane sent the Beatles to Key West.

But Hurricane Dora was a serious natural disaster.

As the band waited in the Keys, Dora approached northeast Florida. The hurricane struck near St. Augustine on September 10, producing destructive winds, coastal flooding and widespread damage.

The same storm that created an unexpected celebrity spectacle in Key West was creating a genuine emergency hundreds of miles to the north.

And the Beatles' Jacksonville concert remained uncertain.

On to Jacksonville

The Beatles ultimately continued north and performed at the Gator Bowl in Jacksonville on September 11, 1964.

That appearance became historically significant for another reason.

The Beatles opposed performing before racially segregated audiences. Their contract for the Jacksonville appearance stipulated that the group would not be required to perform before a segregated audience, and the Gator Bowl audience was integrated.

So what began as a pop-music tour intersected with another major story unfolding in Florida and the United States: the struggle over racial segregation.

Hurricane Dora, Beatlemania and the civil-rights era all converged in Florida during the same remarkable few days.

Florida History Does Not Always Arrive on Schedule

The September 9 story is a reminder that Florida history is not made only by governors, soldiers, pioneers, railroads and hurricanes.

Sometimes history arrives aboard an airplane that was never supposed to land there.

Hurricane Dora changed a flight plan.

A world-famous band landed in Key West before dawn.

Fans somehow learned they were coming and crowded the airport in the middle of the night.

Four young musicians who had expected to be in Jacksonville instead found themselves staying at a motel on South Roosevelt Boulevard.

And for two days, Key West became an accidental stop on the Beatles' first great American tour.

September 9 in Florida History

There was no Beatles concert in Key West on September 9, 1964.

There did not need to be.

The story was the arrival itself—the collision of a major hurricane, an island city and a cultural phenomenon unlike anything American popular music had experienced before.

Florida's weather had rewritten the itinerary.

And because it did, the State Archives of Florida now preserves photographs of John Lennon, Paul McCartney, George Harrison and Ringo Starr standing outside a Key West motel where none of them had planned to be.

September 9, 1964: Hurricane Dora sent the Beatles to Key West—and Florida history gained one remarkable detour.
