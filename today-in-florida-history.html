August 30 — Discovery Begins a Record-Setting Career: Space Shuttle Discovery launched from Kennedy Space Center on STS-41D in 1984. The six-day mission deployed three communications satellites and tested a 102-foot experimental solar array, beginning the most-flown career in the shuttle fleet.

August 30 — The Land Office Behind Florida's Pioneer Settlement, 1842

On August 30, 1842, Congress created the Alachua land district and ordered a federal land office established at Newnansville. Coming just weeks after the Armed Occupation Act, the law helped build the administrative machinery through which public land could become legally recorded farms, cattle ranges, homesteads, and communities.

August 29 — Gemini V Returns from Its Record-Setting Mission, 1965

On August 29, 1965, astronauts Gordon Cooper and Pete Conrad returned to Earth after nearly eight days in orbit. Gemini V had launched from Cape Kennedy, Florida, and helped prove that astronauts and spacecraft could endure a mission lasting approximately as long as a journey to the Moon and back.

August 28 — Menéndez Reaches Florida and Names San Agustín, 1565

On August 28, 1565, Spanish admiral Pedro Menéndez de Avilés reached the Florida coast during the expedition that would establish St. Augustine. The date was the feast day of Saint Augustine of Hippo, giving San Agustín the name that has endured for more than four and a half centuries.

August 27 — Tallahassee Bus Boycott Leaders Arrested, 1956

On August 27, 1956, Tallahassee police arrested Rev. C.K. Steele and Rev. Daniel B. Speed for operating the carpool that helped sustain the Tallahassee Bus Boycott.
