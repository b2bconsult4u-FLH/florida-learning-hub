President George W. Bush was visiting Emma E. Booker Elementary School when he learned that the United States was under attack and delivered his first public statement from the Sarasota school.

Hours after Hurricane Dora battered northeast Florida, the Beatles reached Jacksonville and performed at the Gator Bowl before an integrated audience.

September 10 — Jean Ribault Sails from Fort Caroline, 1565

Jean Ribault sailed south with much of Fort Caroline's fighting force to attack newly established St. Augustine. A hurricane scattered and wrecked the French fleet, giving Pedro Menéndez de Avilés the opportunity to march north and destroy the weakened French colony.

Read September 10: Ribault and French Florida

September 10 — Hurricane Donna Strikes the Florida Keys, 1960

One of Florida's great twentieth-century hurricanes crossed the Middle Keys during the early morning with estimated sustained winds near 140 mph before continuing across the peninsula.

September 10 — Hurricane Dora Makes Landfall Near St. Augustine, 1964

The hurricane that diverted the Beatles to Key West made landfall shortly after midnight near St. Augustine, bringing destructive wind, coastal flooding and extraordinary rainfall to northeast and north Florida.

Read September 10: Hurricane Dora

September 10 — Hurricane Irma Hits Florida, 2017

Irma made landfall at Cudjoe Key as a Category 4 hurricane with 130-mph winds, then struck near Marco Island later that afternoon and affected communities across much of the state.

Read September 10: Hurricane Irma

September 9 — Big Joe 1 Tests Mercury's Heat Shield, 1959

NASA launched an uncrewed full-scale Mercury test capsule from Cape Canaveral to prove the ablative heat shield that would protect America's first astronauts during reentry.

Read September 9: Big Joe 1

September 9 — Hurricane Dora Sends the Beatles to Key West, 1964

Hurricane Dora blocked the Beatles' planned flight to Jacksonville and sent the world's most famous band to Key West instead, where they landed before dawn and spent an unexpected two days at the Key Wester Motel.

Read September 9: The Beatles in Key West

September 9 — The Stono Rebellion and the Road to Freedom in Spanish Florida, 1739

The Stono Rebellion began in South Carolina as enslaved freedom seekers marched south toward Spanish St. Augustine. Their intended destination reveals the extraordinary importance of Fort Mose and Spanish Florida as an early refuge from British colonial slavery.

Read September 9: Stono and Spanish Florida

September 9 — The Great 1919 Florida Keys Hurricane

A powerful major hurricane battered Key West and the Florida Keys with destructive winds before crossing the Gulf of Mexico. The storm became a major maritime disaster and one of the deadliest hurricanes of its era.

Read September 9: The 1919 Hurricane

September 8 — St. Augustine Is Established, 1565

Pedro Menéndez de Avilés formally claimed Florida and began establishing San Agustín beside the Timucua community of Seloy. The settlement survived war, imperial rivalry and centuries of change to become America's oldest enduring European-founded city.

Read September 8

September 7 — The Sunshine Skyway Opens Across Tampa Bay, 1954

The original two-lane Sunshine Skyway created a permanent highway connection across lower Tampa Bay. Its history would span postwar growth, a parallel bridge, the catastrophic 1980 Summit Venture collision, and the construction of today's cable-stayed Florida landmark.

Read September 7

September 6 — Miami Dedicates Its Skyscraper Courthouse, 1928

Thousands gathered as Miami formally dedicated the 28-story Dade County Courthouse, a boom-era government skyscraper that became one of South Florida's defining civic landmarks.

September 5 — Greater Miami Is Created, 1925

During the height of Florida's land boom, Miami expanded to absorb Coconut Grove, Silver Bluff, Allapattah, Lemon City, Buena Vista and Little River, transforming a collection of neighboring communities into what was called Greater Miami.
