September 9 — Big Joe 1 Tests Mercury's Heat Shield, 1959: NASA launched an uncrewed full-scale Mercury test capsule from Cape Canaveral to prove the ablative heat shield that would protect America's first astronauts during reentry.

September 9 — Hurricane Dora Sends the Beatles to Key West, 1964

Hurricane Dora blocked the Beatles' planned flight to Jacksonville and sent the world's most famous band to Key West instead, where they landed before dawn and spent an unexpected two days at the Key Wester Motel.

Read September 9: The Beatles in Key West

September 9 — The Stono Rebellion and the Road to Freedom in Spanish Florida, 1739

The Stono Rebellion began in South Carolina as enslaved freedom seekers marched south toward Spanish St. Augustine. Their intended destination reveals the extraordinary importance of Fort Mose and Spanish Florida as an early refuge from British colonial slavery.

Read September 9: Stono and Spanish Florida

September 9 — The Great 1919 Florida Keys Hurricane

A powerful major hurricane battered Key West and the Florida Keys with destructive winds before crossing the Gulf of Mexico. The storm became a major maritime disaster and one of the deadliest hurricanes of its era.

Read September 9: The 1919 Hurricane

September 8 — St. Augustine Is Established, 1565

Pedro Menéndez de Avilés formally claimed Florida and began establishing San Agustín beside the Timucua community of Seloy. The settlement survived war, imperial rivalry and centuries of change to become America's oldest enduring European-founded city.

Read September 8

September 7 — The Sunshine Skyway Opens Across Tampa Bay, 1954

The original two-lane Sunshine Skyway created a permanent highway connection across lower Tampa Bay. Its history would span postwar growth, a parallel bridge, the catastrophic 1980 Summit Venture collision, and the construction of today's cable-stayed Florida landmark.

Read September 7

September 6 — Miami Dedicates Its Skyscraper Courthouse, 1928

Thousands gathered as Miami formally dedicated the 28-story Dade County Courthouse, a boom-era government skyscraper that became one of South Florida's defining civic landmarks.

Read September 6

September 5 — Greater Miami Is Created, 1925

During the height of Florida's land boom, Miami expanded to absorb Coconut Grove, Silver Bluff, Allapattah, Lemon City, Buena Vista and Little River, transforming a collection of neighboring communities into what was called Greater Miami.

Read September 5

September 4 — The Labor Day Hurricane Strikes Florida — Again, 1935

Two days after devastating the Middle Keys, the 1935 Labor Day Hurricane made a second Florida landfall near Cedar Key. The story explores Cedar Key's fishing-community setting, storm surge, 1935 construction and the very different warning systems available at the time.

Read September 4

September 3 — Roosevelt Backs the Cross-Florida Ship Canal, 1935

President Franklin D. Roosevelt announced $5 million in federal funding to begin the long-discussed Cross-Florida Ship Canal, launching a project that would later become one of the state's most important environmental battles and ultimately help create the Marjorie Harris Carr Cross Florida Greenway.

Read September 3

September 2 — The Labor Day Hurricane Devastates the Florida Keys, 1935

A Category 5 hurricane struck near Islamorada, killing more than 400 people—including approximately 259 veterans and other federal highway workers—and ending Henry Flagler's Overseas Railway.

Read September 2

September 1 — The SS Tarpon Sinks in the Gulf, 1937

The longtime Panhandle steamship sank approximately eight nautical miles off Panama City. Thirteen of the 31 people aboard survived; 18 died.

Read September 1

August 31 — A Storm Begins Its March Toward Tragedy, 1935

A tropical depression formed near the southeastern Bahamas. Two days later, the Category 5 Labor Day Hurricane devastated the Middle Keys, killing more than 400 people—including approximately 260 World War I veterans—and ending operations on Henry Flagler's Overseas Railway.

Read August 31

August 30 — Discovery Begins a Record-Setting Career, 1984

Space Shuttle Discovery launched from Kennedy Space Center on STS-41D. The six-day mission deployed three communications satellites and tested a 102-foot experimental solar array, beginning the most-flown career in the shuttle fleet.

Read August 30: Discovery

August 30 — The Land Office Behind Florida's Pioneer Settlement, 1842

Congress created the Alachua land district and ordered a federal land office established at Newnansville. Coming just weeks after the Armed Occupation Act, the law helped build the administrative machinery through which public land could become legally recorded farms, cattle ranges, homesteads, and communities.

Read August 30: Land Office

August 29 — Gemini V Returns from Its Record-Setting Mission, 1965

Astronauts Gordon Cooper and Pete Conrad returned to Earth after nearly eight days in orbit. Gemini V had launched from Cape Kennedy and helped prove that astronauts and spacecraft could endure a mission lasting approximately as long as a journey to the Moon and back.

Read August 29

August 28 — Menéndez Reaches Florida and Names San Agustín, 1565

Spanish admiral Pedro Menéndez de Avilés reached the Florida coast during the expedition that would establish St. Augustine. The date was the feast day of Saint Augustine of Hippo, giving San Agustín the name that has endured for more than four and a half centuries.

Read August 28

August 27 — Tallahassee Bus Boycott Leaders Arrested, 1956

Tallahassee police arrested Rev. C.K. Steele and Rev. Daniel B. Speed for operating the carpool that helped sustain the Tallahassee Bus Boycott.
