Today in Florida History · November 20, 1861

A Governor’s Letterbook Records Florida’s Wartime Lighthouse Concerns

By Wm. E. McMullen II

The State Archives of Florida dates an entry in Governor John Milton’s letterbook to November 20, 1861. The catalog indexes it under coastal defense, sabotage and lighthouse infrastructure. Those subjects belong together in a wartime record. On a coast used by both merchant vessels and naval ships, a light could help a captain locate an inlet or follow a route after dark. Control over the light gave wartime authorities control over a piece of that navigation system. [1] [2]

Florida’s Civil War Heritage Trail describes what happened during 1861. Confederate authorities and sympathizers extinguished or disabled lights along parts of the coast. Federal control remained in places including the Keys and Dry Tortugas. The result was a coast where control of particular towers depended on who could reach them and keep them operating. [2]

A separate report preserved by Florida Memory describes actions at Jupiter Inlet and Cape Florida. Its authors wrote to Governor Madison Starke Perry and described removing equipment and damaging the Cape Florida light. Their letter shows how participants explained their own actions. Read alongside the heritage survey, it also shows that a lighthouse could be put out of service without bringing its tower down. The equipment inside mattered as much as the structure visible from offshore. [2] [3]

November 20 dates the Milton record in the archives. The incidents described in the separate report relate to earlier actions in 1861. Keeping those dates and documents separate gives readers a better picture of how wartime decisions moved from coastal stations to the governor’s files. [1] [3]

Classroom connection

Explain how taking a light out of service could affect both a warship and a civilian crew. Why should a historian keep the two letters separate?
