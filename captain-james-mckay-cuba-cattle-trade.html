Captain James McKay and the Cuba Cattle Trade

The Scottish Mariner Who Played Both Sides to Keep His Cattle Trade Alive

Florida Learning Hub · Mini Article · Florida Cattle & Maritime History

In 1863, Confederate officials put one of Tampa's wealthiest citizens on trial for treason. Captain James McKay had spent years running the Union naval blockade to supply Confederate forces, yet he had also made a secret arrangement with the Union Navy, trading discounted beef for safe passage of his Cuba-bound cattle shipments. His story is complicated, and understanding Florida's cattle economy means understanding those contradictions.

McKay is credited with opening Florida's first organized cattle trade with Cuba, creating a commercial route that helped shape Tampa and Florida ranching for generations.

McKay's story is not a simple tale of pioneer enterprise. It is a complicated story of cattle, shipping, war, and survival.

From Scotland to Tampa

Born in 1808 in Thurso, Scotland, James McKay spent years at sea before immigrating to the United States in 1836. He and his wife, Matilda Alexander Cail, arrived in Tampa in 1846, when the settlement was still very small. McKay quickly became involved in commerce, real estate, sawmilling, shipping, and civic life.

Building Florida's Cuba Cattle Trade

McKay's vessels carried cargo through the Gulf and Caribbean before he began organizing cattle shipments to Cuba in the late 1850s. In 1859 he chartered the steamer Magnolia for regular runs. The trade linked Florida cattle country to Cuban markets and established a model later expanded by other cattlemen and shipping interests.

Mayor, Blockade Runner, Accused Traitor

McKay became Tampa's sixth mayor in 1859. During the Civil War, he opposed secession at first but ultimately supported the Confederacy and used the sidewheeler Scottish Chief in blockade-running operations. At the same time, he arranged to supply beef to Union troops at Fort Jefferson in exchange for protection for some of his cattle shipments. Once discovered, that arrangement led to a Confederate treason trial.

After the war, McKay resumed cattle and shipping activity until his death in 1876. His influence continued through later Florida cattle and shipping enterprises, including the rise of the Lykes family.

Why This Mattered for Florida

McKay's Cuba trade helped create an economic template that later Florida cattlemen followed. His wartime dealings with both sides also show how tightly commerce, politics, military power, and survival could overlap in Civil War-era Florida.

Did You Know?

  • McKay was born in Scotland and became a major Tampa businessman and civic leader.
  • He was tried for treason by Confederate authorities after his dealings with Union forces became known.
  • His Cuba cattle trade helped establish a commercial route later used by other major Florida cattle interests.

Vocabulary

  • Blockade runner — a ship, or its operator, that evades a naval blockade to deliver goods.
  • Charter — to hire a vessel and crew for a specific purpose or period.

Discussion Questions

  • How did the Cuba cattle trade change opportunities for Florida ranchers?
