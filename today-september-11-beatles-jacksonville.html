The Beatles Play an Integrated Jacksonville Concert

By Wm. E. McMullen II

September 11, 1964 — The Beatles performed before an integrated audience at Jacksonville's Gator Bowl after publicly refusing to appear before a segregated crowd.

The concert occurred only hours after Hurricane Dora battered northeastern Florida. The storm had diverted the Beatles' flight to Key West, disrupted electrical service throughout Jacksonville and left strong winds behind. When the band finally performed, gusts reportedly approached 40 miles per hour.

The official Beatles history records that they played to an integrated crowd while Dora was still tearing through the region. The band's position against segregated performances became part of its subsequent American concert contracts.

An Extraordinary Three-Day Florida Story

  • September 9 — Hurricane Dora diverts the Beatles to Key West.
  • September 10 — Dora strikes northeastern Florida near St. Augustine.
  • September 11 — The Beatles reach Jacksonville and perform at the Gator Bowl.

Together, the events connect hurricane history, Florida civil rights and one of the defining cultural phenomena of the 1960s.
