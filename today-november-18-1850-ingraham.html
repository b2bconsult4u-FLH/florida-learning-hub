Today in Florida History · November 18, 1850

James Ingraham Is Born, Years Before His Everglades Survey

By Wm. E. McMullen II

James Edmundson Ingraham was born November 18, 1850, in Dartford, Wisconsin. His Florida story began after his education in Wisconsin and his move south in 1874. He eventually became president of the South Florida Railroad Company and took part in an expedition that looked at how a railroad could reach the state’s lower peninsula. [1] [2]

In March 1892, a survey party crossed from Fort Myers to Miami to investigate a possible railroad route for Henry B. Plant’s system. A route had to be judged by more than the distance between two towns. Survey work helped identify what lay in between and gave railroad leaders a basis for deciding where to put their money. The surviving expedition account allows readers to follow that practical problem from the perspective of people making the crossing. [1] [2]

Ingraham reported that an east-coast route was more practicable. Henry Flagler subsequently hired him, and Ingraham took on responsibilities for land holdings and later became an officer of the Florida East Coast Railway. A railroad company’s decisions about land gave its executives a role in development as well as transportation. The work put Ingraham at the intersection of route planning and the disposition of property as the railroad expanded. [1]

The birthday offers an opening into a chapter of Florida history usually told through the famous names of Plant and Flagler. Ingraham’s record brings the survey party into view. Before anyone could put a railroad on a timetable, someone had to gather information about the route and explain what the findings meant for construction. [1] [2]

Classroom connection

What information would an 1892 survey party need before recommending a route? Separate what could be read on an existing map from what would require field observations.
