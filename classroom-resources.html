My Research Project Logbook

Book Description

Turn a research assignment into an organized journey from the first idea to the final presentation.

My Research Project Logbook is a step-by-step research guide and working journal designed for students in Grades 5–8. It gives young researchers one place to choose a topic, develop a focused question, find and evaluate sources, take organized notes, verify information, build citations, develop conclusions, and prepare a finished project or presentation.

Rather than simply collecting facts, students learn the habits of careful research: asking good questions, evaluating evidence, comparing sources, taking notes in their own words, checking important claims, and documenting where information came from.

Inside students will find:

  • Topic brainstorming and project-planning pages
  • Research-question development
  • Background knowledge and vocabulary activities
  • Keyword and search-term planning
  • Primary and secondary source guidance
  • Source credibility and evaluation checklists
  • 12 detailed source-record pages
  • 20 structured research-note pages
  • Paraphrasing and quotation guidance
  • Working bibliography and citation planning
  • Source comparison and corroboration activities
  • Claim-Evidence-Reasoning exercises
  • Perspective, purpose, and bias analysis
  • Thesis and central-idea development
  • Project outlining pages
  • Project progress logs
  • Fact-checking worksheets
  • Names, dates, statistics, and accuracy checks
  • Visual-source and image-use guidance
  • Drafting, revision, editing, and peer-feedback checklists
  • Presentation planning and practice
  • Final project checklist and reflection pages

A dedicated section also helps students use search engines, digital resources, and AI tools responsibly, emphasizing verification, original thinking, and following teacher or school requirements.

Ideal for research papers, history projects, social studies, science, geography, biographies, literature projects, homeschool assignments, independent research, and classroom project-based learning.

Part of the Florida Learning Hub Classroom Resources series.

My STEM Engineering Design Logbook

Book Description

Turn an idea into a solution through the engineering design process.

My STEM Engineering Design Logbook is a guided student workbook for Grades 5–8 that takes young engineers from identifying a problem through designing, building, testing, improving, and presenting a solution.

Students learn to ASK, RESEARCH, IMAGINE, PLAN, CREATE, TEST, ANALYZE, IMPROVE, RETEST, and COMMUNICATE while documenting the evidence and decisions behind their designs.

Inside, students work with problem statements, users and stakeholders, measurable criteria, constraints, safety and risk, environmental impacts, research, brainstorming, concept sketches, decision matrices, trade-offs, technical drawings, materials and costs, prototype construction, fair testing, quantitative data, graphs, failure analysis, redesign, optimization, engineering ethics, and presentation skills.

Dedicated build logs, test sessions, redesign pages, engineering notebook pages, and reusable STEM challenges provide plenty of room for authentic project work.

Students are also introduced to responsible use of digital tools and AI in engineering, with an emphasis on verification, safety, original decision-making, and following teacher and school requirements.

Ideal for STEM classrooms, science and engineering projects, makerspaces, homeschool programs, project-based learning, engineering clubs, and independent student projects.

Part of the Florida Learning Hub Classroom Resources series.

My Nature & Environmental Science Field Journal

Book Description

Take science beyond the classroom and into the natural world.

My Nature & Environmental Science Field Journal is a guided student workbook for Grades 4–8 that teaches young scientists how to observe, investigate, measure, record, compare, and explain what they discover outdoors.

Students develop real field-science skills while exploring habitats, weather, water, soil, plants, wildlife, biodiversity, ecosystems, environmental change, and human impacts. They learn to distinguish observations from inferences, collect qualitative and quantitative data, make scientific sketches, design field investigations, organize measurements, graph results, evaluate evidence, and draw responsible conclusions.

Inside are dedicated field-observation logs, organism records, data tables, graphing pages, Florida ecosystem studies, and ready-to-use environmental investigations. Students also learn responsible wildlife observation, field safety, environmental stewardship, data integrity, and appropriate use of digital tools and AI.

Designed for flexible use in classrooms, homeschool programs, environmental education, nature centers, field trips, science clubs, parks, schoolyards, and independent outdoor investigations.
