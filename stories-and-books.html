Captain Jim McMullen and the Pinellas Peninsula

Before Pinellas County had cities, highways, and subdivisions, pioneers like Captain Jim McMullen were helping shape the peninsula.

Captain Jim McMullen and the Pinellas Peninsula tells the story of an early Florida pioneer whose life became intertwined with the development of the region that would eventually become Pinellas County.

A mariner, cattleman, farmer, and community leader, Captain Jim lived during a period when Florida’s Gulf Coast was still largely defined by open land, scattered settlements, waterways, cattle ranges, and emerging citrus groves. His story provides a window into the everyday realities of building a life and a community on the nineteenth-century Florida frontier.

From cattle and agriculture to transportation, settlement, and community development, this book explores Captain Jim’s life within the larger transformation of the Pinellas Peninsula. It also examines the people, places, and activities that connected isolated pioneer homesteads and helped lay the foundations for communities that followed.

Carefully researched and richly illustrated with historical photographs, archival material, and interpretive imagery, Captain Jim McMullen and the Pinellas Peninsula brings one Florida pioneer’s story into the broader history of Florida’s Gulf Coast.

It is a story of family, persistence, opportunity, and a rapidly changing peninsula—and of the generations who helped build Florida long before the landscape became familiar to us today.

Part of the Florida History Series — Before There Were Highways and Subdivisions.

Daniel “Uncle Dan” McMullen and the Building of Largo

Long before the Pinellas Peninsula became one of Florida’s most densely populated regions, pioneers like Daniel McMullen were helping shape its economy and communities.

A member of one of the area’s early pioneer families, McMullen built his life around the industries that defined nineteenth-century Florida: cattle, citrus, land, and local commerce. His story reflects the transformation of the Pinellas frontier from scattered homesteads and open-range cattle country into a developing network of farms, groves, businesses, and settlements.

Daniel McMullen explores the life and work of a Florida pioneer whose contributions reached beyond a single occupation. As a cattleman, citrus grower, merchant, and community figure, he participated directly in the economic growth that helped transform the Pinellas Peninsula.

Part of the Florida History Series — Before There Were Highways and Subdivisions, this book preserves the stories of the people, industries, and communities that built Florida before modern highways, subdivisions, and cities reshaped the landscape.
