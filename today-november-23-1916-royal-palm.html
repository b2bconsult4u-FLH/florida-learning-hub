Today in Florida History · November 23, 1916

Royal Palm State Park Is Dedicated at Paradise Key

By Wm. E. McMullen II

On November 23, 1916, Royal Palm State Park was dedicated at Paradise Key. The area contained a hammock whose variety of plants drew the attention of scientists and preservation advocates. The dedication was also an occasion to mark completion of the Ingraham Highway from Florida City to Paradise Key, giving visitors a way to reach the new park. [1] [2]

The Florida Federation of Women’s Clubs took up the campaign for protection. Its preservation chair, May Mann Jennings, helped lead the effort. The National Park Service describes an initial state allocation of 960 acres to the federation and a matching contribution from Mrs. Flagler. The land and the advocacy behind its protection turned a campaign to preserve Paradise Key into a park with acreage set aside for the purpose. [1]

Protecting the hammock and building a road served different needs. One set land aside. The other gave people a route to see it. Their joint dedication illustrates how organizers presented conservation to the public. A visitor could learn what the clubs and scientists valued by going to the place itself. [1] [2]

Royal Palm later became part of Everglades National Park. Its earlier history is worth seeing on its own terms. In 1916 the focus was a particular place and a particular landscape. The surviving park records show how scientific interest, civic organizing and access came together well before the national park’s dedication in 1947. [1] [2]

Classroom connection

Choose one contribution each from scientists, the women’s clubs and the highway builders. How did those contributions serve different parts of the project?
