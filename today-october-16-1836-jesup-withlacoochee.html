General Jesup Takes Command South of the Withlacoochee

1836 — Thomas Sidney Jesup recorded assuming command of U.S. troops south of the Withlacoochee River.

By Wm. E. McMullen II

A Short Diary Entry Marks a Change

On October 16, 1836, General Thomas Sidney Jesup recorded in his campaign diary that he had assumed command of U.S. troops south of Florida's Withlacoochee River. The State Library and Archives of Florida preserves his diary, which offers an unusually direct view of the daily decisions and logistical demands of the Second Seminole War.

A Limited Command

The geographical qualification matters. Jesup did not assume command of every U.S. force in Florida on October 16. Governor Richard Keith Call continued directing troops elsewhere in the territory. Jesup learned in late November that he had been appointed to overall command; the formal transfer occurred in December 1836.

A War Over Removal

The Second Seminole War, which began in 1835, arose from federal efforts to force Seminole people and their allies from Florida to lands west of the Mississippi River. Seminole resistance, including resistance by Black Seminoles, challenged United States military plans and its removal policy. At its center were communities fighting to remain in their homeland.

The Work Behind a Campaign

The October 16 diary entry also recorded requests for pack animals, corn, tools, lumber, boats, and other supplies. Such lists show the logistical effort required to move troops through Florida's terrain. The Withlacoochee River and surrounding wetlands were central to the military geography of the conflict.

Whose Perspective Survives?

Jesup's diary is a primary source, but it is the account of a U.S. military commander. Its language reflects his mission and assumptions. The people he was ordered to remove had their own political goals, leaders, families, and reasons for resisting. An accurate history uses the diary to document the Army's actions without allowing the Army's viewpoint to become the only viewpoint.

Why October 16 Matters

A brief entry from 1836 captures the beginning of Jesup's limited regional command and opens a much larger story about forced removal, resistance, and the practical challenges of war in Florida. Its precise wording guards against treating his October assignment as the date he took command of the entire Florida campaign.
