Today in Florida History · November 30, 1817

The Scott Party Is Attacked on the Apalachicola

By Wm. E. McMullen II

On November 30, 1817, Seminole warriors attacked a U.S. Army party led by Lieutenant Richard W. Scott as it traveled up the Apalachicola River toward Fort Scott. The attack near present-day Chattahoochee killed soldiers and civilians, including children. A historical marker in Chattahoochee commemorates the event, and the Florida Historical Society places it in the chain of violence that led to a wider war. [1] [2]

The river was a route for moving supplies and people toward the fort. It connected settlements and military posts across a region where the border between the United States and Spanish Florida carried political significance. That made a trip on the river part of a larger dispute over authority and movement along the frontier. The party’s destination was upstream, while the attack took place in what is now Florida. [1] [2]

The Florida Historical Society describes the attack as retaliation for a U.S. attack on Fowltown, a Seminole community in southern Georgia, about a week earlier. The Scott party’s losses helped prompt a larger American military response. U.S. operations led by Andrew Jackson reached villages in Spanish Florida in what became known as the First Seminole War. [1]

Local commemoration and a regional history answer different questions. The marker helps a reader identify the place associated with the attack. The society’s account connects that place to Fowltown and later operations. Reading them together gives the November 30 event a geographic setting and a place in the sequence of decisions that widened the conflict. [1] [2]

Classroom connection

Sketch the relationship between the Apalachicola River, Fort Scott and the site near Chattahoochee. Then explain how putting the attack after Fowltown changes the way you understand the event.
