Today in Florida History · November 19, 1969

Apollo 12 Lands Near Surveyor 3

By Wm. E. McMullen II

Five days after launching from Kennedy Space Center, Apollo 12’s lunar module landed in the Ocean of Storms on November 19, 1969. Pete Conrad and Alan Bean went down to the surface while Richard Gordon remained in lunar orbit. Their target was near Surveyor 3, a robotic spacecraft already on the Moon. The ability to reach that particular area was an important part of the mission. [1] [2] [3]

A launch carries a crew onto a trajectory. A landing requires control over where that journey ends. The Surveyor site gave NASA a fixed target against which to judge Apollo 12’s landing accuracy. Once on the surface, the astronauts could walk to the earlier spacecraft. They removed several parts for return to Earth, giving researchers material that had been exposed to conditions on the Moon. [1] [3]

The surface work also included collecting samples and setting up experiments. Gordon’s role in orbit remained part of the same operation. The lunar module’s ascent stage later had to rendezvous with the command module before the crew could return. The whole mission depended on the work of the astronaut who stayed in orbit as well as the two who reached the ground. [1] [3]

Florida’s November 14 edition follows the launch through its lightning disturbances. This November 19 entry follows a different milestone of the same flight. The two dates bracket different problems: getting the spacecraft safely on its way and putting a crew close enough to a specific lunar target to do planned work on foot. [1] [3]
