Today in Florida History · November 28, 1964

Mariner 4 Leaves Florida for Mars

By Wm. E. McMullen II

On November 28, 1964, Mariner 4 launched from Complex 12 at Florida’s Cape Canaveral on an Atlas-Agena rocket. The spacecraft was bound for a flyby of Mars. Its twin, Mariner 3, had launched earlier that month but failed to get free of its protective shroud. For Mariner 4, the launch team used a different shroud. This time the spacecraft left Florida successfully and continued toward Mars. [1] [2]

The launch began a journey of months. During the July 1965 encounter, Mariner 4’s camera recorded images as it passed the planet. NASA describes how the first image data came back as numbers. Team members assembled strips of those numbers and shaded them by hand while waiting for computer processing. It was a practical way to see the emerging picture from the spacecraft’s signal. [1]

The photographs showed impact craters in the terrain that the camera had passed over. The mission also measured properties of the Martian atmosphere by tracking how radio signals behaved during the encounter. Those two kinds of observations answered different questions. Images showed features on the surface, while radio measurements provided evidence about the atmosphere that future Mars missions would have to pass through. [1]

November 28 marks the launch from Florida. The flyby and return of the data were later milestones. The story connects the work of a launch team, the performance of a small spacecraft on a distant flight, and the people who turned a stream of numbers back into pictures on Earth. [1] [2]

Classroom connection

Why did the team have to turn numbers into shades? How did a radio measurement reveal something a photograph could not?
