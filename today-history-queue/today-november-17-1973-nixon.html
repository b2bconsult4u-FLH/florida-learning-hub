Today in Florida History · November 17, 1973

Nixon Says “I Am Not a Crook” at Walt Disney World

By Wm. E. McMullen II

At 7:02 p.m. on November 17, 1973, President Richard Nixon began taking questions in the Ballroom of the Americas at Walt Disney World’s Contemporary Hotel. John C. Quinn, president of the Associated Press Managing Editors Association, introduced him. The session went out live on radio and television. Before it ended, Nixon would deliver the five words that came to define the evening: “I am not a crook.” [1] [2]

The line came during a discussion of Nixon’s finances. Before taking the next editor’s question, he returned to an earlier exchange about his tax payments. He addressed questions about how he could afford his real estate holdings, including two houses in Florida that he described as an office and a residence. His official diary identifies the Key Biscayne compound as the place he had begun the day. [1] [2]

Nixon attributed his wealth in part to writing a book and practicing law between his vice presidency and presidency. He cited proceeds from selling stock and his New York apartment. Then he addressed the television audience and denied both profiting from public service and obstructing justice. The famous declaration followed those denials. The transcript records what Nixon said about his finances and conduct. Those claims can be checked against other records. [2]

Watergate had already come up in the first question. The break-in at Democratic National Committee headquarters had occurred in June 1972. A fight over White House recordings had led to the firing of special prosecutor Archibald Cox on October 20, 1973. Leon Jaworski became special prosecutor on November 1, a little over two weeks before Nixon met the editors. [2] [3]

In July 1974, the Supreme Court upheld a subpoena for White House tapes. A June 23, 1972 recording, made six days after the burglary, showed Nixon engaging in obstruction of the investigation, according to the National Archives. That evidence directly contradicted his November denial in Florida. Nixon resigned on August 9, 1974. [3] [4]

The White House diary puts the end of the session at 8 p.m. Nixon took photographs with editors, then left for McCoy Air Force Base and flew back to South Florida. The diary can tell a reader where he was and when he left. The transcript and the later tapes let readers compare what he said in Florida with evidence that emerged afterward. [1] [2] [4]

Classroom connection

Identify one claim about Nixon’s finances and one about his conduct. Which claim does the June 23 recording address?
