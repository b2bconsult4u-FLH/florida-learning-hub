Today in Florida History · November 29, 1961

Enos Orbits Earth Aboard Mercury-Atlas 5

By Wm. E. McMullen II

On November 29, 1961, the Mercury-Atlas 5 mission launched from Cape Canaveral Air Force Station with a chimpanzee named Enos aboard. The flight put the Mercury spacecraft into orbit and let mission controllers evaluate how it performed during an orbital mission. This was a step in the test program that preceded an American astronaut’s first orbit of Earth. [1] [2]

The plan called for three orbits. The spacecraft developed difficulty with its attitude control system, which governed its orientation. Controllers returned the capsule after two orbits. The congressional spaceflight chronology records that the retrorockets fired on the second orbit instead of the planned third. In a test program, that difference matters: the number of orbits achieved and the reason for returning early are both part of the result. [1] [2]

Mercury-Atlas 5 gave teams experience operating and monitoring the capsule during orbit and making a return decision when conditions changed. It also connected the Florida launch site to a recovery operation that had to begin when the mission came down. An orbital flight involved work well beyond the moment of liftoff, and the timing of that work could change because of what controllers learned from the spacecraft. [1] [2]

The mission raises a question about how we describe a test. Enos reached orbit and the capsule returned; the flight also ended a circuit earlier than planned. Both facts belong in the account. Together they give a better picture of what the Mercury team accomplished and what the equipment did in use. [1] [2]

Classroom connection

What does attitude control do? Why should a flight summary record both the planned number of orbits and the number completed?
