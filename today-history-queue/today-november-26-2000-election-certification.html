Today in Florida History · November 26, 2000

Florida Certifies Its Presidential Vote

By Wm. E. McMullen II

On Sunday, November 26, 2000, Florida’s Elections Canvassing Commission certified its presidential results. The state legislative report records 2,912,790 votes for the Republican presidential electors and 2,912,253 for the Democratic electors. The certified difference was 537 votes. That small number came from subtracting two statewide totals, each the product of returns from Florida’s counties. [1]

Certification marked the point at which the state formally adopted those totals. In the days leading up to it, courts considered which amended returns could be included and when counties had to submit them. The Florida Supreme Court set a November 26 filing deadline for amended certifications. The commission’s announcement followed the deadline that had become part of the dispute. [1]

The parties continued litigating after November 26. Later legal proceedings concerned disputed ballots and the conditions for a recount. On December 12 the U.S. Supreme Court issued its decision in Bush v. Gore. Both dates belong to the same election, but each marks a different action by a different body. The state commission certified returns in November, while the Supreme Court resolved the recount dispute in December. [1] [2]

Florida’s election records offer a way to follow the story through institutional roles. Counties submitted returns, a state commission certified the statewide numbers, and courts decided contested legal questions. Reading the certification and the later decision in order makes it possible to see where the tally ended and where the fight over reviewing it continued. [1] [2]

Classroom connection

Calculate the certified margin yourself. Then name the body that certified the returns and the court that decided the later federal case.
