The 31st Infantry Division Is Activated at Camp Blanding

By Wm. E. McMullen II

On November 25, 1940, the U.S. Army activated the 31st Infantry Division at Camp Blanding in Florida. The date came more than a year before the attack on Pearl Harbor. The Eisenhower Presidential Library’s unit records connect the activation to Executive Order 8594, dated November 16. The interval between the order and activation marks a concrete step in bringing National Guard formations into federal service. [1] [2]

Known as the Dixie Division, the 31st included National Guard units from several states. Bringing such units into one division required them to operate within a single command. A camp provided a place to assemble and train under that command. Camp Blanding’s role in the story was to provide the site where the division entered federal service, before it eventually went overseas. [1]

The date also mattered to the people preserving the camp’s history. A newspaper account in the University of Florida’s collection reported plans to dedicate a museum and memorial at Camp Blanding on November 25, 1990, fifty years after the activation. The article explains why organizers chose the date. It illustrates how a military event can become a date of local remembrance long after the troops move on. [2]

November 25 belongs to the history of American preparation for World War II. The division’s case gives readers a specific Florida place, an order and an activation date. Together, these records show one way the Army grew before the United States formally entered the war. [1] [2]
