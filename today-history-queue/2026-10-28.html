Air Mail Launches Pan Am’s Scheduled Service from Key West

1927 — A regular mail connection between Key West and Havana helped establish Pan American Airways as a scheduled airline.

By Wm. E. McMullen II

The Importance of a Schedule

On October 28, 1927, Pan American Airways began scheduled service between Key West and Havana. The University of Miami Libraries describes a mail-carrying flight across the roughly ninety-mile route. The Pan Am Historical Foundation identifies the date as a cornerstone in the airline’s development: a regular operation, rather than a single successful flight, was beginning.[1][2]

Two October Flights to Keep Apart

An earlier flight on October 19 had met a critical mail-contract deadline using a borrowed aircraft. October 28 belongs to the start of the regular service. Those events are close enough to be confused, but they answer different questions. One shows how an enterprise managed to begin; the other shows how it turned a first accomplishment into an operating route. Accounts of later passenger service add another milestone and should not be folded into the mail flight’s date.[2]

A Postal Route as a Business Foundation

A passenger can decide not to travel; a mail service must organize around an agreed route and delivery obligation. That difference helps explain why a sack of letters could matter to the beginnings of an airline. The University of Miami’s archival collection includes a folder specifically describing the first daily round-trip Key West–Havana service, with original air mail dated October 28–29, 1927. Such objects connect a broad corporate history with evidence of actual transport.[1][3]

Florida Looks Across the Straits

Key West’s location made the route a compact international connection. An overseas destination did not have to be on another continent: Cuba lay close enough to make a relatively short flight a crossing between nations. The route places Florida in a history of communications as well as tourism. Letters, schedules, airfields, and crews all belonged to the work behind the journey, long before an international flight became an ordinary travel purchase.

From a Flight to an Institution

The University of Miami preserves corporate records and other materials documenting Pan Am’s long life. Those collections encourage a useful change in perspective. Famous later aircraft and distant routes can make the airline’s beginnings seem inevitable. October 28 returns the story to a small route and a practical task: carrying mail reliably between two places. A schedule was a promise to repeat the journey, and repetition was what made a service.[1]
