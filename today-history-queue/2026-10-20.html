The Plane Crash That Shook Jacksonville’s Southern Rock Legacy

1977 — A crash in Mississippi took six lives and forever changed the story of a band born in Jacksonville.

By Wm. E. McMullen II

A Florida Story, a Mississippi Tragedy

On October 20, 1977, the chartered aircraft carrying Lynyrd Skynyrd crashed near Gillsburg, Mississippi. The flight was traveling from Greenville, South Carolina, to Baton Rouge, Louisiana. Although the accident occurred outside Florida, its consequences reached directly into Jacksonville, where the band had formed. Six of the 26 people aboard died; twenty survived with injuries. The National Transportation Safety Board’s report supplies the official account of the flight and investigation.[1]

The People Behind the Headlines

Those killed included singer Ronnie Van Zant, guitarist Steve Gaines, backing vocalist Cassie Gaines, assistant road manager Dean Kilpatrick, and the two pilots. The loss came shortly after the release of Street Survivors. Naming the people matters: a familiar anniversary can become a shorthand about a famous band while obscuring the individual lives lost and the survivors’ injuries.[1][2]

Jacksonville Before the Arena Lights

The group’s own history traces its beginnings to teenagers Ronnie Van Zant, Bob Burns, and Gary Rossington in Jacksonville in 1964. Allen Collins and Larry Junstrom joined the developing group, which performed under several names before becoming Lynyrd Skynyrd. Its eventual mixture of blues, country, and rock carried a local musical partnership into a much wider audience. Florida’s Department of State recognizes that Jacksonville origin in its Florida Artists Hall of Fame account.[2][3]

What the Investigation Established

The NTSB attributed the accident to fuel exhaustion and total loss of engine power, tied to the crew’s inattention to the fuel supply. It also identified inadequate flight planning and an engine malfunction associated with increased fuel consumption among the contributing circumstances. That finding is more useful than treating the disaster as an unexplained twist of fate. The technical record and the band’s remembrance answer different questions: one examines how the flight failed; the other follows the people and music.[1]

A Legacy That Continued

The band’s history records a return in 1987 with Ronnie’s younger brother Johnny Van Zant singing lead. Its later recognition included induction into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame in 2006. October 20 remains a day of loss, but Florida’s connection is also a story of musical beginnings, collaboration, and endurance. Remembering the crash accurately does not require allowing it to become the only chapter in Jacksonville’s Southern rock history.[2]
