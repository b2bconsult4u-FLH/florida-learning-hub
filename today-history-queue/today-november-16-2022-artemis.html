Artemis I Lifts Off from Kennedy Space Center

By Wm. E. McMullen II

At 1:47 a.m. Eastern Standard Time on November 16, 2022, NASA’s Space Launch System lifted off from Launch Complex 39B at Kennedy Space Center. It carried Orion on Artemis I, an uncrewed lunar flight. The launch was the first chance to see the rocket, spacecraft and upgraded ground systems perform together on a mission. Florida supplied the launch site and the ground facilities that made the test possible. [1] [2]

The launch was the first test in a sequence. The rocket had to send Orion on its way. Orion then had to operate away from Earth, travel beyond the Moon and return. The mission timeline follows those steps across 25 days. A spectator watching from Florida could see the departure, but mission teams could judge the outcome only as each later stage was completed. [1] [2]

Orion’s path took the spacecraft into an orbit that carried it thousands of miles beyond the Moon. It then came home at a speed far above that of a commercial airliner, making reentry an important part of the test. The mission ended in a splashdown on December 11. NASA’s timeline records a reentry speed of about 24,581 miles per hour. The ocean recovery furnished the final milestone in a flight whose opening minutes had been visible from the Florida coast. [1] [2]

That sequence explains why NASA calls Artemis I an integrated test. A spacecraft can be examined on the ground and individual systems can be tested under controlled conditions. A complete mission asks whether those systems work together in the order and under the conditions the flight requires. November 16 marks the day that question could finally be answered in flight. [1] [2]
