Today in Florida History · November 27, 1879

Governor Drew Names Florida’s Thanksgiving Date

By Wm. E. McMullen II

Thursday, November 27, 1879, was the Thanksgiving date Governor George F. Drew named for Florida. The surviving state document is dated November 13. The two dates tell us when the governor issued the proclamation and when he asked Floridians to observe the holiday. In the same year, President Rutherford B. Hayes named November 27 for the national observance. [1] [2]

Looking at the two proclamations together shows how an observance could be addressed to two overlapping audiences. The federal document spoke to the country. Drew’s document spoke to Florida. Both identified the same Thursday. A reader in Florida therefore had a state document and a federal document pointing to the same day. [1] [2]

Each proclamation gives historians a record of official language and intentions. It also raises questions that a proclamation cannot answer on its own. Local newspapers might describe which shops closed or when communities held services. Family letters might describe how a household spent the day. A statewide invitation establishes a date and a public purpose, while records made closer to home can show how people acted on it. [1] [2]

Florida Memory preserves the state record alongside other holiday proclamations. The 1879 example lets students use the document as a starting point for researching public custom. They can find the date in the text, compare it with the federal counterpart, and then choose a Florida community whose newspapers might show the observance in practice. [1] [2]

Classroom connection

Read both proclamations. What do they establish about the calendar? Name one question about a local observance that would require a different source.
