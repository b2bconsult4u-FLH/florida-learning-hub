Today in Florida History · November 21, 1914

The Miami Herald Reports the Marianna School Fire

By Wm. E. McMullen II

On November 21, 1914, the Miami Herald reported a deadly fire at the Florida Industrial School for Boys in Marianna. The fire had occurred early on November 18. A later review of newspaper accounts identified eight students and two staff members who died. The November 21 report is a record of when news of the deaths reached readers of that newspaper. [1] [2] [3]

The Herald account printed the substance of a telegram sent to the family of student Joe Wetherbee. For a family receiving such a message, it carried news of a child’s death. For investigators many decades later, the newspaper preserved part of a communication that could be compared with other accounts. The same document could serve both purposes because someone put the message into print and the paper survived. [1] [3]

Researchers consulted other newspapers and an account filed with a state board. The investigators reported that they found no independent confirmation of the names of those killed in the school ledgers they examined. That gap made the newspapers and other records especially important. Comparing several surviving reports helped the researchers distinguish information that appeared across accounts from details printed only once. [2] [3]

This story begins with a dated publication and follows the records behind it. The fire, the telegram and the published report belong to a sequence. Remembering the eight boys and two staff members also means keeping the evidence about their deaths clear enough for later readers to examine. [1] [2] [3]

Classroom connection

Which source would help establish when the fire happened? Which would show when the Miami Herald told its readers? Explain the value of comparing newspapers with institutional records.
