Mary Lee Graham Opens a Door in Key West's Business Leadership

October 8, 1953 — Mary Lee Graham became the first woman elected president of the Key West Chamber of Commerce.

By Wm. E. McMullen II

A First for the Island City

On October 8, 1953, Mary Lee Graham became the first woman elected president of the Key West Chamber of Commerce. The Florida Keys History Center records the milestone in its daily chronology. It was one chapter in a civic career that linked business, historic preservation and public service.

Graham owned Lee's Orient Restaurant. Her chamber leadership placed a local businesswoman at the head of an organization representing the island's commercial community. The significance rests in the documented first, without needing to invent an election-night scene or words she might have spoken.

Protecting the City's Past

The City of Key West credits Graham as a charter member of the Old Island Restoration Foundation and with helping preserve the Armory and West Martello Tower. Her work connected the island's future with the survival of its older buildings.

Preservation is a form of civic decision-making. A building must have people willing to argue for its value, organize support and keep working after the first proposal. Graham's record offers a useful example of leadership extending beyond a single office.

More Places to Serve

In 1971 she became the first woman to serve on Key West's planning and restoration board. The governor appointed her to the Key West Historic Preservation Board in 1972. She also served for more than a decade on the Monroe County Library Board and began serving as a city commissioner in 1977.

These appointments and responsibilities show the range of her interests. Business organizations, libraries, preservation groups and city government all offered ways to shape the community. Her chamber presidency was an early milestone in that broader work.

Why It Matters

Key West's history is more than a sequence of famous visitors and waterfront landmarks. It includes residents who built institutions and protected the places later generations would inherit.

Remembering October 8 brings one of those residents into view. Graham's career connects women's participation in business leadership with the practical work of maintaining a city's cultural life. Her achievements make a local election worth remembering well beyond the day the votes were counted.
