Today in Florida History · November 24, 1901

Miami’s First Baptist Congregation Uses Its New Chapel

By Wm. E. McMullen II

On Sunday, November 24, 1901, Miami’s First Baptist congregation held its first services in its new chapel. Five days later, the Miami Metropolis reported on the occasion. The Florida Baptist Historical Society’s account identifies the Gothic building with the congregation led by pastor W. E. Stanton. The newspaper record gives the opening service a date, while later accounts help explain what happened to the building. [1] [2]

The services marked the beginning of regular use of a particular church home. Local history places the chapel on land donated by Henry Flagler. Its site was near what are now Northeast First Avenue and Northeast Fifth Street. That modern reference lets a reader connect the young city’s church history with streets a person can locate on a present-day map. [1] [2]

Buildings and congregations do not always share the same address for their whole lives. The historical society records that the chapel was later moved to Buena Vista, where it was renamed Stanton Memorial Church in 1916. A search confined to its original lot would miss that chapter in its history. The building’s relocation also gives a historian another place to look for photographs, property records and newspaper accounts. [1]

A single milestone can leave several dates in the record. November 24 was the service. November 29 was the newspaper’s report. The move and renaming came much later. Putting those dates in order lets readers follow the building through Miami’s changes and see how a congregation and a structure can each have a history of their own. [1] [2]
